
## ITC Last-Mile Project — How to Talk About It Naturally

---

## YOUR 30-SECOND ANSWER TO "TELL ME ABOUT YOUR PROJECT"

Practice this until it sounds like *you're* saying it, not reading it:

> *"So I did a project on last-mile distribution for ITC FMCG — specifically the rural side. It actually started from something I observed personally — I was in a village in UP and the local kirana store didn't stock Aashirvaad atta at all, even though ITC is supposed to have a huge rural network. The shopkeeper said he'd ordered it once, it never came, so he switched brands. That stuck with me.*
>
> *So I dug into it — looked at ITC's distribution model, compared it with what HUL and Dabur are doing in rural markets, and tried to figure out where the supply chain is actually breaking down. I found four main gaps: a 12-day replenishment cycle in rural vs 4–6 days urban, a fill rate of around 78% (so 1 in 5 orders doesn't get fulfilled), zero demand data below the stockist level, and a very high stockout rate — about 20% in rural markets.*
>
> *I proposed a five-lever solution I called REACH — Rural hubs in each district, Village Entrepreneurs through the government's CSC network, a basic app for demand signals, cold chain co-investment, and formalising the informal sub-stockist layer. The financial estimate works out to roughly ₹400–470 crore investment over 3 years, unlocking maybe ₹2,500–3,500 crore of incremental revenue by Year 3."*

## LIKELY QUESTIONS AND HOW TO ANSWER THEM

*(These are meant to sound like YOU talking, not a prepared script. Adjust the words to your natural style.)*

---

**"Why ITC specifically?"**

> *"Honestly, ITC interested me because of the paradox. They have e-Choupal — they've been in rural India for procurement for over 20 years, farmers know the ITC name. But when it comes to selling FMCG back to those same villages, the supply chain doesn't match up to that brand trust. That gap between the rural equity ITC has earned and how it's being used commercially felt like an interesting problem."*

---

**"What is the REACH model?"**

> *"It's five things — I named it REACH mostly as a memory device. R is Rural Hub-and-Spoke: setting up dedicated rural distribution hubs in 150 priority districts, one per district, which formally manage the sub-stockist layer below them. E is enlisting Village Entrepreneurs — India's government has 6.5 lakh CSC centres in gram panchayats already, each run by a local entrepreneur. Instead of ITC building a last-mile network from scratch like HUL did with Shakti, they can plug into existing infrastructure. A is automating the demand signal — just a basic mobile app for sales reps to capture stock levels at outlets, which creates visibility that doesn't exist today. C is cold chain co-investment at those rural hubs, mostly relevant for future fresh/dairy products. And H is formalising the sub-stockist layer — getting their names in a system, giving them a territory and a target and a small bonus structure."*

---

**"How is this different from HUL's Shakti?"**

> *"The philosophy is similar — get a local person who knows the village to handle last-mile. But HUL built Shakti from zero in 2001. They spent years recruiting and training 1.7 lakh women entrepreneurs. ITC doesn't need to repeat that. The CSC network already exists in every gram panchayat, the VLEs are already there and already earning income from other services. ITC just needs to add product ordering as one more thing the VLE can do through their existing interface. It's a faster and cheaper path to the same outcome."*

---

**"What's the biggest problem you identified?"**

> *"Honestly, the zero visibility below the stockist level. That surprised me the most. ITC has data at the Regional DC level, data at the Preferred Stockist level. But below that? Nothing. The sub-stockist is completely invisible — no system record, no name, no transaction data. So the whole supply chain from RDC downward is basically pushing products on guesswork about what rural India needs. That's why fill rates are 78% and stockouts are 20%. You can't fix what you can't see."*

---

**"How did you come up with the financial numbers?"**

> *"I want to be upfront — these are estimates, not audited figures. ITC doesn't break out rural-specific data in their annual reports. I used a bottom-up approach: for new outlet coverage, I took the number of new outlets per Rural Hub (based on Dabur's cluster model data from trade publications) times average rural outlet annual purchase value (I used ₹60,000–80,000, which is a rough benchmark from distributor economics I found cited in financial publications). For fill rate improvement, I calculated the revenue that 14% more fulfilled orders represents at ITC's rural business run-rate. For VLEs, I used ₹15,000 per month per VLE — conservative relative to HUL Shakti benchmarks. I applied 38% gross margin which I took from ITC's annual report. I made sure to say in the report that these numbers could be wrong in either direction — the pilot is the only real validation."*

---

**"What would you prioritise if you had to pick one thing?"**

> *"The sub-stockist formalisation. It costs almost nothing — just the time to get their names in a system and set up a basic incentive structure. But the impact is immediate: ITC discovers for the first time who is actually moving its products in rural India, and those people now have a financial reason to perform. Every other lever in the framework works better once you have accountability at the sub-stockist level. I'd start there tomorrow."*

---

**"What could go wrong?"**

> *"The risk I worry about most is speed. HUL and Dabur are actively signing up good rural distributors with formal agreements right now. The best sub-stockists and rural operators in UP and Bihar aren't unlimited. If ITC takes 2 years to start, the most capable rural partners will already have HUL or Dabur agreements. The REACH framework only works if ITC moves fast enough to lock up the right partners before competitors do. The other real risk is the VLE programme — government partnerships sound elegant in a report but move slowly in practice. I'd want a dedicated team managing the CSC partnership, not leaving it as a side project."*

---

**"Did you talk to anyone on the ground?"**

> *"Informally, yes. The whole project started from a conversation with a kirana shopkeeper in Azamgarh, UP. I also spoke to a few people who work in FMCG distribution (not ITC specifically) to cross-check some of the assumptions I was making about fill rates and replenishment cycles. The quantitative data comes from public sources — Nielsen, company annual reports, trade publications — but the qualitative understanding of where the actual friction points are came from those conversations."*

---

## THREE THINGS THAT WILL MAKE YOU SOUND GENUINE IN THE ROOM

**1. Own the data limitations.**
Don't say "ITC's rural fill rate is 78%." Say "ITC's rural fill rate is estimated at around 78% based on distributor interviews cited in trade publications — ITC doesn't publish this number directly." That shows you actually read the sources rather than copied a slide.

**2. Have a personal reason for the project.**
The Azamgarh kirana store story is your anchor. Use it at the start and come back to it at the end. It makes the whole project feel grounded in a real observation rather than a textbook exercise.

**3. Be honest about what you'd actually do first.**
Most students present a complex 36-month roadmap as if they believe every step. Say instead: "If I'm being practical, I'd do two things in the first 90 days — start sub-stockist formalisation because it costs almost nothing and creates accountability overnight, and run a 50-hub pilot in UP to validate the financials before committing to a full national rollout." That sounds like someone who's actually thought about implementation.

*Remember: the goal in the interview isn't to prove you have all the answers. It's to show you asked good questions, did genuine research, and can reason about a real problem. If someone pushes back on a number, say "that's a fair challenge — the estimate depends on X assumption, and I'd validate it with the pilot data."*
