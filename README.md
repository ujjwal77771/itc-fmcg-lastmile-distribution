# ITC FMCG Last-Mile Distribution: Solving the Rural Reach Problem

> *A supply chain strategy project for campus placement preparation*

---

## What This Project Is About

This project looks at why ITC FMCG — despite having a ₹20,000 crore business and deep rural trust through e-Choupal — struggles to reliably stock its products in rural kirana stores.

It started from a personal observation: a kirana store in a village in Azamgarh, UP had stopped ordering Aashirvaad atta because it never arrived on time, so the shopkeeper switched to a competitor brand. That one conversation raised a bigger supply chain question.

**The core problem:** ITC reaches ~2.4 million rural outlets directly, against a total market of ~9–10 million rural kirana stores. Its rural fill rate sits around 78%, replenishment cycles average 12 days, and it has zero visibility into demand below the Preferred Stockist level.

---

## The Proposed Solution — REACH Framework

| Lever | What it does |
|---|---|
| **R** — Rural Hub-and-Spoke | 150 dedicated district-level hubs, formalising the sub-stockist layer |
| **E** — Village Entrepreneurs (CSC) | Partner with India's 6.5 lakh CSC gram panchayat centres as order points |
| **A** — Automated Replenishment | Basic mobile app for Sales Reps to generate demand signals from villages |
| **C** — Cold Chain Co-investment | Micro cold storage at Rural Hubs via NHM subsidy scheme |
| **H** — Hyperlocal Sub-Distributor Formalisation | Register, territory-map and incentivise informal sub-stockists |

**3-Year Targets:**
- Rural outlet reach: 2.4M → **3.8M**
- Distributor fill rate: 78% → **92%**
- Replenishment cycle: 12 days → **6 days**
- Estimated incremental revenue by Year 3: **₹2,500–3,500 Cr**
- Investment: **~₹400–470 Cr** | Payback: **~18–24 months**
